- **S1** breaks the story or security. **S2** a visible logic or immersion break. **S3** polish or rare.
- Each finding has an ID (ST story, WD world, OR orders, TX text, UI interface, SV server, CI tests). Fix by ID.
- File paths under **Where** are leads, not verdicts. `engine/`, `shared/`, `ui/` and `map3d/` are under `public/js/`; `server/`, `tests/`, `scripts/` and `public/data/` are from the repo root. Line numbers are approximate (the code at `f65be14`).

## Story and canon

What the chronicle says happened, against what the engine did.

### ST1 · S1 · Story beats are all headlined "Grave news reaches <place>"

In a 30-turn mock game, 28 of 30 canon-beat cards read "Grave news reaches Castle Black / House Targaryen / King's Landing..." with the subtitle "The ravens carry the word across the realm". Bran's fall, Drogo's wedding, the assassin's knife, Jon taking the black and Catelyn's arrest of Tyrion all look the same. The facts already carry the real titles ("The boy who fell", "A Dothraki wedding", "A knife in the night", "The court goes south") and the full tale. On Maester-12B the Chronicle shows the same generic headline and subtitle, with the true text behind Details, and the turn digest repeats "Grave news reaches Winterfell" twice for two different events.

**Where:** `engine/facts/heads.js` beatHead (~876) handles only the King's ride stages progress and arrival; canon_beat summary (~1130) is a fixed sentence. Any game, any house, from turn 3.

**Repro:** node scripts/bughunt/probe-corpus.mjs stark 100 30 30d corpus.txt, then look at the [canon_beat] lines in .bughunt-out/corpus.txt. Live view: docs/screens/bughunt/st1-chronicle-grave-news.jpg and docs/bughunt/live-samples/play-stark-30d-turns.txt (turn 3).

### ST2 · S2 · A tourney is won on the day it is called, and the guests ride to it afterwards

The tourney action picks a champion and records the call and the result in one step. The lords of the region only start riding for it after that. In one 20-turn game, 11 of 13 tourneys were called and won in the same turn, and 14 lords set out for a tourney 5 to 25 days after it had ended (Ardrian Chyttering left on day 107510 for Dragonstone's tourney, which ended on 107505). Same family of mistake as the King holding a tourney from the road.

**Where:** `engine/actions/court.js` tourney() ~66; `shared/retinues.js` (the guests).

**Repro:** node scripts/bughunt/analyze-game.mjs stark 100 20 30d (rules tourney-instant, ride-to-ended-tourney).

## Server and saves

The local web server and what it accepts.

### SV1 · S1 · Any web page can reconfigure the game

A cross-site form POST with a plain-text body changed the model server URL. The server reads the body as JSON whatever the content type, has no origin or host check, no CORS handling and no security headers. The saved API key is sent to whatever URL is configured, so a page you visit could redirect your prompts and your key. It matters most if the server is ever reachable from another machine.

**Where:** `server/index.js` POST /api/config; `server/llm.js` 195, 245.

**Repro:** node scripts/bughunt/probe-api.mjs (lines [cors], [host-header], [headers]).

### SV2 · S2 · No lock per save

Two End turns sent together both ran (the turn advanced by two). Undo, talk, edits to orders, another advance and even delete were accepted while a long jump was running on the same save.

**Where:** `server/game.js` advance; jump jobs in `server/index.js`.

**Repro:** node scripts/bughunt/probe-api.mjs ([concurrent-advance]) and node scripts/bughunt/probe-race.mjs.

### SV3 · S2 · Bad requests and broken saves

Bad or null JSON gives a 500 with the raw error text. A corrupt save is listed but cannot be opened, with no explanation. Asking for facts with a huge limit never returns. An OPTIONS request hangs with no reply. An oversized request is refused with 413 and leaves the connection stuck for the next request.

**Where:** probe-api.mjs, probe-api2.mjs.

**Repro:** node scripts/bughunt/probe-api.mjs and probe-api2.mjs.

### SV4 · S3 · Inputs are not checked

Spans such as "abc", "-5d", "0d", "9999d" are accepted, "999999999d" ran for 78 seconds, "1y" took 68 seconds on the mock. A 5 MB chronicle and a 100 KB message were accepted.

**Where:** advance span parsing.

**Repro:** node scripts/bughunt/probe-api.mjs ([span]).

### SV5 · S3 · Every response carries the whole game

The saved game is 882 KB and is sent with every talk, advance and undo, even when the reply is empty. It includes the random generator state, which lets a client predict outcomes.

**Where:** viewOf and state responses.

**Repro:** node scripts/bughunt/probe-api2.mjs ([view]).

## Not yet looked at

The hunt was wide, not exhaustive. Things nobody has tried, in the order I would try them:

- A long live-model run (30 or more turns) for narration drift, repeated phrases, letters and audiences that contradict the state. Owner only (needs the model); the live harnesses are in `scripts/bughunt/live/`.
- Whether the **narrator and the headline writer** ever contradict a fact (dates, places, who is alive) on the mock: `npm run coherence` and `bench/lib/coherence.js` are the tools; run them over 100-turn games, not 30.

## Method, for the record

Seven kinds of probe, all in `scripts/bughunt/`: invariants and text scans on every turn of long mock games (`sweep-soak`, `analyze-game`); the starting data of every house (`sweep-world`); a corpus dump of everything a player reads, read by eye (`probe-corpus`); the server's edges with hostile requests (`probe-api*`, `probe-race`); the interface in a real browser under SwiftShader (`sweep-ui`, `probe-ui2`, `probe-zoom`, `probe-houses-ui`, `probe-leak`, `probe-life`); the order reader against a battery of phrases (`probe-orders`, `live/live-orders`); and live-model play (`live/`).
